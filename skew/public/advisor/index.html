Method

How Skew decides

  1. Universe. All S&P 500 members. Fundamentals come from each company's annual 10-K filings via SEC EDGAR (5 years, split-adjusted). Prices, analyst consensus, earnings history and insider/Congress activity come from the Stocks-Research daily snapshot; final prices are refreshed live from Nasdaq.
  2. Quality score (0–100). Four equal pillars: growth (3-yr revenue and EPS CAGR), quality (operating margin, FCF margin, margin trend), balance sheet (debt/equity) and valuation (PEG). Missing data scores zero.
  3. Asymmetry. 5-year bear/base/bull prices: EPS grows at min(g/2, 8%) / min(g, 20%) / min(g, 35%) a year, then re-rates to a P/E of min(P/E, 15) / 25 / 35. Asymmetry = bull upside ÷ bear downside.
  4. AI analyst. For the shortlist, GPT reads numbered facts — the metrics, the latest 8-K earnings release, news and market data — and returns earnings takeaways, moat, bull/bear points and thesis breakers, each citing its facts. It may adjust conviction by ±10 points; it never produces the numbers.
  5. Allocation. Final score = quality score + AI adjustment + asymmetry bonus (up to 10). The index core gets its share; the top picks split the rest in proportion to score, capped per stock and per sector. Whole shares only.
  6. Memo check. Every number in the AI-written memo is matched against the facts it was given; anything unmatched is flagged.

Limitations: past growth does not guarantee future growth; scenario multiples are simple rules, not forecasts; US-listed 10-K filers only.
